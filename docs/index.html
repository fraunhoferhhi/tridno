Tri-DNO Trilateral Diffusion Noise Optimization

Abstract

Generating realistic 3D human object interactions (HOI) is essential for applications, such as augmented or virtual reality (AR/VR) or character animation of interactive entertainment. A particular challenge lies in the faithful generation of hand grasps in human object interaction (HOI) scenes, where even minor geometric inconsistencies, such as insufficient contact or interpenetration, can significantly compromise realism. While diffusion-based generative models have shown promising results in HOI synthesis, ensuring physically plausible hand–object interactions remains challenging.

We present Tri-DNO, a diffusion noise optimization strategy to sample from a pretrained multimodal HOI diffusion model, improving hand-object contact. Rather than modifying or retraining the generative model, our approach iteratively optimizes the initial noise of the diffusion generator, thereby generating firm hand object contact, while mitigating penetration and keeping the hand position realistic. Experiments on the GRAB dataset demonstrate improvements over the baseline in grasp quality without additional model training or increased computational cost compared to existing methods. We further demonstrate the applicability of our approach to practical media production scenarios.

Method overview

Tri-DNO builds on TriDi, a trilateral diffusion model of humans, objects, and interactions. A deterministic DDIM sampler maps initial noise to a clean 3D scene. We evaluate the scene with interaction-aware geometric objectives, backpropagate through the sampler, and update the initial noise. The pretrained model stays fixed.

The user chooses which modalities to generate and which to provide as conditions. For example, Tri-DNO can generate a human pose for a fixed object, or place an object into a fixed human grasp.

Results

TriDi can produce plausible overall poses while leaving objects floating beside the hand or fingers penetrating their surface. Tri-DNO reduces these artifacts, improving the geometry of the grasp.

Grasp validity is assessed by visual inspection of 80 scenes per method across eight objects. A valid grasp holds the object at a semantically plausible location, without noticeable finger penetration. Severe penetration (PEN-X) is the proportion of evaluated scenes with penetration deeper than 15 mm.

Application results

Generated interactions can be transferred to a digital human avatar learned from volumetric capture. Here, the avatar holds a phone in a scene rendered in the Unity game engine, demonstrating a practical application for games and media production.
